# My World

## Register

product

## Users

My World is designed for Singapore secondary students who need a low-pressure
way to capture lived school experiences, reflect on what those experiences
might mean, review the evidence, and notice patterns over time.

The public FAQ has a different primary audience: MOE and DXD colleagues,
leadership reviewers, and educators. Many will also read it as parents. They
need to understand the prototype quickly, inspect the team's reasoning, and
see what remains unproven before deciding whether a structured pilot is worth
pursuing.

## Product Purpose

My World is a working prototype that explores whether multimodal capture and
evidence-grounded sensemaking can support more self-directed reflection. It is
one touchpoint in a student's wider support network, not a replacement for
family conversations, teachers, counsellors, or other trusted adults.

Success is not maximum time spent or daily retention. Success would mean that
students can capture meaningful experiences with little friction, recognise
patterns without being assigned a fixed identity, and return to human
conversations with more language and evidence. Whether the prototype can do
this safely and usefully is still a question for research and a carefully
bounded pilot.

## Brand Personality

Warm, inquisitive, and accountable.

The product should feel encouraging without becoming attention-seeking. The
FAQ should feel candid rather than defensive: clear about what is built,
careful about what early signals may mean, and explicit about what the team
still needs to learn.

## Anti-references

- Social feeds, streak mechanics, leaderboards, variable rewards, and
  engagement loops designed to maximise time spent.
- AI companions presented as friends, therapists, authorities, or substitutes
  for trusted people.
- Generic gamified-edtech pages with trophy language, cartoon achievement
  badges, or competitive progress.
- Corporate FAQ pages that bury uncertainty in long prose or present product
  intentions as evidence.
- Generic AI landing pages with gradients, glass cards, vague promises, and
  inflated claims.
- Literal copies of Fauna Robotics' identity, logo, robot imagery, typography,
  palette, or compositions. Its geometric warmth and interaction confidence
  are references, not assets to reproduce.

## Design Principles

1. **Human relationships come first.** Frame My World as a bridge into
   reflection and conversation, never as a substitute for people.
2. **Show the evidence boundary.** Separate product facts, external research,
   early field signals, team commitments, and unanswered questions.
3. **Let readers inspect the reasoning.** Make concerns scannable first, then
   reveal a short answer and an optional deeper evidence view.
4. **Design for agency, not engagement.** Calm motivation, easy stopping, and
   student review matter more than return frequency or time spent.
5. **Treat criticism as a signal.** Invite questions, record uncertainty, and
   connect each concern to what the team would test before a pilot.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG 2.2 AA for the FAQ and product-facing web controls. Every interaction
must work with keyboard and touch, expose clear focus and state, preserve
readability at zoom, and support reduced motion. Flip cards require an
equivalent non-spatial reading order and must not leave hidden controls
focusable. Product media needs captions or concise text equivalents and a
non-autoplay path. Language should remain understandable to colleagues,
educators, parents, and students without assuming specialist AI knowledge.
